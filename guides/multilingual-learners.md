# **Newcomer English Language Development**

| Course Title | Newcomer English Language Development |
| :---- | :---- |
| **Department** | Multilingual Learners (ML) |
| **Course Description** | Newcomer English Language Development is an English language development course based on the WIDA English Language Development Standards. It is designed for students who are beginning to learn English, generally have been in the United States for less than one year, and score below 2.0 on the WIDA ACCESS or WIDA Screener. Students develop foundational English language skills for daily communication and school success. Instruction focuses on listening, speaking, reading, and writing, with an emphasis on social and instructional language and basic communication in academic settings. Students develop the language needed to participate in high school and access learning in mathematics, science, social studies, and language arts. Students begin to narrate, inform, explain, and argue through familiar topics and supported classroom activities. This course may be taken for one year. |

# **Intermediate English Language Development**

| Course Title | Intermediate English Language Development |
| :---- | :---- |
| **Department** | Multilingual Learners (ML) |
| **Course Description** | Intermediate English Language Development is an English language development course based on the WIDA English Language Development Standards. It is designed for students who generally score 3.0–3.9 on the WIDA ACCESS or WIDA Screener. Students develop academic English language skills, critical thinking, and interpersonal communication across content areas. Instruction focuses on listening, speaking, reading, and writing for academic purposes, including the language of mathematics, science, social studies, and language arts. Students interpret and analyze information, communicate ideas, explain concepts, and support opinions with relevant details and evidence. Students strengthen their ability to narrate, inform, explain, and argue in both interpretive contexts (listening, reading, and viewing) and expressive contexts (speaking, writing, and representing). This course may be repeated for multiple years. |
